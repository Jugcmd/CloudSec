1.     Assessment Brief Overview
For software developers, understanding and leveraging cloud technologies is increasingly essential. This summative assignment encompasses all elements taught in the “Cloud Engineering” module. Using cloud platforms and services, it challenges you to demonstrate their understanding of cloud architecture, deployment strategies, security practices, and scalability techniques.
Learning Outcomes (LOs)
1.         Justify choices of cloud infrastructure, cloud deployment models, and cloud services with regard to business and legal requirements. 
2.         Design and build cloud-based solutions that meet the needs of businesses and organisations. 
3.         Optimise cloud-based solutions, including monitoring and performance optimisation, cost optimisation, and automation and orchestration. 
4.         Design and implement secure and compliant cloud-based systems and applications to ensure cloud security and compliance. 
5.         Select and defend cloud technology for a given purpose utilising trends in cloud computing, including emerging technologies and best practices. 
 
Summative Assessment Overview
The task is to create and deploy a small application to the cloud, utilising cloud architecture as appropriate, and to demonstrate the application, design, and rationale (20 minutes maximum)
Formative assessment will take the form of iterative development during the sessions, there is no formal submission point, nor should you submit your work for feedback. 2.     Summative submission
Project Description
This practical assessment focuses on designing and implementing a cloud-based application. You will apply your knowledge of cloud architecture, security, performance optimisation, and cost management to develop a robust and scalable solution.
 
You should create a small application that would assist someone(s) with a task at your workplace. You should keep the application small but it should include, as part of its function:
·         A GUI
·         Some business logic code
·         A database
 
The demonstration should clearly articulate the problem in the organisational context and make use of a range of appropriate sources to support your arguments. It should be pitched as a demonstration of the cloud principles utilised, with rationale.
Your explanations should be provided as verbal explanations (voice over). You don’t need to have your camera on. The demonstration should be pitched as a demo, not a powerpoint presentation, although diagrams for illustration purposes are acceptable.
Demonstration Structure
Your demonstration should include the following aspects of your cloud-based application:
1.       Cloud Architecture Design: Design the cloud architecture for the application.
o   Use appropriate cloud services (compute, storage, networking).
o   Include diagrams and detailed descriptions of the architecture.
o   Justify the choice of services and architecture design.
o   Environmental impact of your solution
2.           Security and Compliance Implementation: Implement security measures to protect user data and ensure compliance with regulations.
o   Implement encryption for data at rest and in transit.
o   Use Identity and Access Management (IAM) to control access.
o   Ensure compliance with GDPR, PCI DSS, and other relevant standards.
3.           Performance Optimisation: Demonstrate implementation of strategies to optimise the performance of the application.
o   Use auto-scaling to handle variable traffic.
o   Implement monitoring tools to track performance metrics.
o   Optimise database queries and application code for performance.
4.           Cost Management: Develop and implement cost management strategies.
o   Use reserved instances and spot instances to reduce costs.
o   Implement auto-scaling to optimise resource usage.
o   Use cost management tools to monitor and control spending.
5.           Application Deployment and Artefact: Deploy the e-commerce application on the chosen cloud platform.
o   Demonstrate the working application
o   Ensure the application is fully functional and meets the requirements.
 
You should ensure you use references throughout to justify your decisions and present in a professional manner.

	Design and architecture (35%)
	The design and architecture are either not provided or are fundamentally flawed. There is no clear structure or rationale behind the design choices. Key components and their interactions are missing or incorrect.
	The design and architecture are minimally developed and poorly structured. Key components are mentioned but not well-defined or integrated. The rationale behind design choices is unclear or poorly justified.
	The design and architecture are partially developed but lack coherence and detail. Key components are identified but not fully explained or integrated. The rationale behind design choices is weak or incomplete.
	The design and architecture are adequately developed with a basic structure. Key components are identified and their interactions are explained. The rationale behind design choices is provided but may lack depth.
	The design and architecture are well-developed and structured. Key components are clearly identified and their interactions are well-explained. The rationale behind design choices is logical and well-justified.
	The design and architecture are comprehensive and detailed. Key components are thoroughly identified and their interactions are clearly and effectively explained. The rationale behind design choices is strong and well-supported.
	The design and architecture are exemplary, highly detailed, and innovative. Key components are meticulously identified and their interactions are seamlessly integrated. The rationale behind design choices is exceptionally well-justified, demonstrating deep insight and understanding.
	 
	Security and compliance (10%)
	Security measures are either not implemented or are fundamentally flawed. There is no consideration of compliance with relevant standards and regulations. The system is highly vulnerable to security threats.
	Minimal security measures are implemented but are largely ineffective. Compliance with relevant standards and regulations is poorly addressed or ignored. The system remains vulnerable to many security threats.
	Some security measures are implemented but are insufficient to protect the system effectively. Compliance with relevant standards and regulations is partially addressed but lacks thoroughness. Significant vulnerabilities remain.
	Basic security measures are implemented and provide a reasonable level of protection. Compliance with relevant standards and regulations is addressed but may lack detail. Common security threats are mitigated, but there may be gaps in the security strategy.
	Security measures are well-implemented and provide good protection against common threats. Compliance with relevant standards and regulations is well-addressed. The system demonstrates secure coding practices, effective encryption, and robust access control.
	Security measures are comprehensive and highly effective. Compliance with relevant standards and regulations is thoroughly addressed. The system is well-protected against a wide range of threats, employing advanced security practices and thorough threat analysis.
	Security measures are exemplary and provide exceptional protection. Compliance with relevant standards and regulations is meticulously addressed. The system is highly resilient to a broad spectrum of threats, employing state-of-the-art security practices, proactive threat detection, and innovative security solutions.
	

	Performance optimisation (20%)
	No performance optimisation strategies are implemented. The system is slow, inefficient, and unable to handle expected loads. There is no evidence of performance monitoring or tuning.
	Minimal performance optimisation strategies are implemented but are largely ineffective. The system struggles with performance issues and cannot handle variable traffic. Performance monitoring is inadequate or absent.
	Some performance optimisation strategies are implemented but are insufficient. The system has noticeable performance issues and limited ability to handle variable traffic. Performance monitoring is basic and lacks depth.
	Basic performance optimisation strategies are implemented. The system performs adequately under normal conditions but may struggle under high load. Performance monitoring is in place but may not be comprehensive.
	Well-defined performance optimisation strategies are implemented. The system performs well under most conditions and can handle variable traffic. Performance monitoring is effective and includes key metrics.
	Comprehensive performance optimisation strategies are implemented. The system performs efficiently under a wide range of conditions and handles variable traffic smoothly. Performance monitoring is thorough and includes advanced metrics and analysis.
	Exemplary performance optimisation strategies are implemented. The system performs exceptionally well under all conditions, including high load. Performance monitoring is state-of-the-art, with real-time metrics, automated alerts, and proactive tuning.
	

	Cost management (20%)
	No cost management strategies are implemented. The system incurs high and uncontrolled costs. There is no evidence of cost monitoring or optimization efforts.
	Minimal cost management strategies are implemented but are largely ineffective. The system incurs unnecessary costs, and there is little to no effort to monitor or control spending.
	Some cost management strategies are implemented but are insufficient. The system incurs avoidable costs, and cost monitoring is basic and lacks depth. There is limited effort to optimize resource usage.
	Basic cost management strategies are implemented. The system incurs reasonable costs, and there is some effort to monitor and control spending. Cost optimization is present but may not be fully effective.
	Well-defined cost management strategies are implemented. The system incurs controlled costs, and there is effective monitoring and optimization of resource usage. Cost-saving measures such as reserved instances and auto-scaling are utilised.
	Comprehensive cost management strategies are implemented. The system incurs optimized costs, with thorough monitoring and control of spending. Advanced cost-saving measures are employed, and resource usage is highly efficient.
	Exemplary cost management strategies are implemented. The system incurs minimal and highly optimized costs. State-of-the-art cost monitoring and control tools are used, with proactive and innovative cost-saving measures. Resource usage is maximized for efficiency and cost-effectiveness.

Academic Skills/Report Writing​
·         This includes the overall structure of the written work, but more than that, it is the ability to synthesise information, present a well-founded and justified argument. The highest marks go to those who critically evaluate and present original ideas. It also includes the form in which the arguments are expressed.
·         Unless the assessment specifically asks them to reflect on their own experiences, the default expectation is that academic reports should be written in the third person.
·         Arguments should be made from a neutral perspective whenever possible, not based on an individual’s opinions.​
·          The referencing marks are for the use of referencing, not just the accuracy of Harvard style. Every point has an accurate and relevant citation, the grading criteria specifies “accurate” – this would be peer review papers, textbooks and such. Not blogs, not Wikipedia. References need to be as close to the original source as possible. It may be suitable to use product documentation, if this is included there needs to be an awareness of the bias of the source. ​
